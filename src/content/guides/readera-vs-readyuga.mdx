If you're reading this, you've already narrowed your choice of Android ebook reader down to two. That's unusual, and it suggests you've done the broader survey: you've looked past the Kindle app, decided against Moon+ Reader (too busy) and Librera (too forgotten), and landed on ReadEra and ReadYuga. The question now is which one.

I'll give you the honest answer in one sentence: if you want a free, calm, mature reader that handles ten plus formats and has its own TTS, pick ReadEra. If you want on-device AI, free unlimited OCR for scanned PDFs, neural voices, and a feature-rich free tier focused on EPUB and PDF, pick ReadYuga. Everything after this paragraph explains why in detail.

Disclosure before you read further: I build [ReadYuga](/apps/pagesage/). I'm not going to pretend otherwise, and I'm not going to twist the comparison to favour my own app. For a large chunk of casual readers, ReadEra is the right answer, and I'll tell you where. Where ReadYuga genuinely wins, I'll say what and why, with specifics you can verify by installing both apps yourself.

## The one-minute answer

Skip the rest of the guide if one of these matches you:

- **You want a free, ad-free reader that opens every format you've ever downloaded.** Install [ReadEra](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=org.readera). It handles EPUB, MOBI, AZW3, PDF, DJVU, FB2, DOC, DOCX, RTF, ODT, TXT, CBR, CBZ, and even files inside ZIP or RAR archives. No ads, no account, no subscription. If you want an iOS version too, ReadEra is on the App Store; ReadYuga is Android-only right now.
- **You want on-device AI (chapter summaries, chat with any passage, contextual word meanings), free unlimited OCR for scanned PDFs, neural voices, and a Focus Beam reading mode, all in one app.** Install [ReadYuga](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.pagesage.app). The free tier is more feature-rich than most paid readers in this category. A single one-time Pro unlock covers every premium feature. No subscription. ([More about ReadYuga →](/apps/pagesage/))
- **You read scanned-only PDFs regularly.** ReadYuga. It has free, unlimited on-device OCR that makes scanned textbooks searchable, highlightable, and audibly readable. ReadEra opens scanned PDFs as images but does not extract text.
- **You read a lot of MOBI, AZW3, DJVU, FB2, or comic archives.** ReadEra. ReadYuga focuses on EPUB and PDF and doesn't open those formats.
- **A lot of serious readers install both.** That's a legitimate answer. ReadEra for the long tail of formats and the tidy library view, ReadYuga for the AI-assisted study sessions and the scanned textbooks.

The rest of this guide walks through the differences in detail.

## How I put this comparison together

This guide draws on three sources: direct hands-on use of ReadYuga (the app I build at TechYuga Labs), [ReadEra's own documentation and feature list on readera.org](https://readera.org/), and the current state of user feedback from Reddit's r/ebooks, the Slant community comparison, and Play Store reviews from the last six months. Where I describe ReadEra's behaviour, the claim either comes from their official site or from aggregated community reports, not from a controlled test session on my own device.

I was deliberate about this. A lot of "I tested every app" comparison posts are either months out of date or quietly copying each other. For a comparison that's useful to someone making a decision today, tracing specific claims to specific sources is more honest than generic testimony about a test setup that may or may not have happened.

Where the two apps overlap (reading EPUBs, PDFs, dictionary, TTS, library management), I've described what each app does and where I think the practical differences land. Where they diverge (on-device AI, free unlimited OCR on scans, format breadth, iOS availability), I've been specific about which app does what, because that's where the "which should I pick" answer lives.

## Reading experience

Both apps get the quiet, essential parts right. You open a book, you swipe or tap to turn pages, you long-press to highlight. Neither nags you to rate, upgrade, or share. Neither shows ads. Neither requires a login.

ReadEra's visual design is probably my favourite in the Android reader category. It is restrained, mature, and fast. The library view looks like a bookshelf without trying to. The Day, Night, Sepia, and Console reading modes cover most lighting conditions tastefully. Collections let you organize by Favorites, To Read, Have Read, or your own thematic bookshelves. It has been iterated for years and shows it, in the best sense.

ReadYuga is more opinionated. There is a continue-reading card at the top of the library, then a grid of covers with a quote strip at the bottom of the home screen. On the page itself, tap-to-define opens a dictionary card directly rather than going through the system text-selection menu. This is faster for vocabulary building and slightly worse for copying long passages (you'd use the explicit selection mode for that). Both apps are configurable; ReadEra leans toward "get out of the way and let me read"; ReadYuga leans toward "help me learn from this book."

Page turn feels similar in both. Reflow mode on a dense academic PDF works in both. Neither is perfect at this; both are usable.

## Dictionary, translation, and vocabulary

ReadEra integrates with your device's dictionary and translation services. The experience is a text selection menu that calls out to the relevant system or installed-app service. It works, especially if you already use a specific dictionary app you like.

ReadYuga ships a built-in offline dictionary that opens inline with one tap on any word. Definitions, part of speech, pronunciation guide, synonyms, and inflection resolution are all shown without a context-switch. Add to vocabulary with one tap, review via spaced repetition over the following days.

ReadYuga also ships offline translation via Google ML Kit across 100+ language pairs, built into the reader. Highlight a sentence, translate, done, no internet required.

Which flow is better depends on your reading style. If you look up words rarely, ReadEra's system integration is enough and feels less heavy. If you look up words often, especially while reading in a non-native language or through older literature with thick vocabulary, ReadYuga's one-tap inline dictionary plus spaced-repetition review turns reading into active study.

## Scanned PDFs and OCR

ReadEra opens a scanned-only PDF by displaying the images. That is correct behaviour for a pure reader. You can read it like a comic. What you cannot do is search it, select text, look up a word, highlight by tapping, or have it read aloud. There is no text layer, so text-dependent features are off the table.

ReadYuga handles this case specifically. The app triages the PDF on import (sampling several pages to detect if there is a usable text layer), and if the file is scanned it offers to recognize the text using on-device ML Kit OCR. Recognition runs in the background while you can continue reading. Once complete, you can tap any word, highlight, search, and use read-aloud. The OCR is unlimited and free in the base app. It is one of the reasons university students pick up ReadYuga for textbook work.

If you don't read scanned PDFs, ignore this entirely. If you read even one scanned textbook, zine, or old document per month, the difference is real.

## Text-to-speech and audio

Both apps have text-to-speech, which I got wrong in an earlier draft of this guide, so I want to be precise.

ReadEra's TTS uses your device's system voices (Google TTS or Samsung TTS, depending on the phone). You get speed adjustment, repeat options, and sentence-level playback. Users praise it as a genuine strength of the app. ReadEra Premium adds background TTS, which keeps the audio playing when the screen locks or you switch to another app. Without Premium, TTS pauses when the screen locks.

ReadYuga's free TTS also uses your device's system voices. With Pro, you unlock neural voices (Lessac, Amy, Ryan via Piper) that run on-device at a quality closer to Audible-level narration than typical system TTS. ReadYuga's audio is designed to be a continuous reading experience: auto page turn, subtitle-style sentence highlighting synced with the voice, and background playback.

So the honest comparison is **voice quality and continuity**, not presence or absence. Both apps read books aloud. ReadEra's TTS is tasteful and free (background TTS with $14.99 Premium). ReadYuga's free TTS is the same quality; ReadYuga Pro's neural voices are higher fidelity and run entirely on your phone with no streaming, no quota, and no subscription.

If audio is a core use case for you (long commutes, cooking, walking), test both free tiers and listen to a few pages. If the system voice bothers you, the neural voice in ReadYuga Pro is the upgrade; if it doesn't, ReadEra Premium's background TTS is the cheaper path.

## On-device AI

ReadEra has no AI. It is not pitched as an AI app, and the maintainers have been clear about keeping the product focused on reading.

ReadYuga is pitched as an AI app, specifically an on-device AI reader. With Pro, you get:

- **Chapter summaries** generated on-device from the chapter you're reading
- **Page Q&A**: ask a question about the current page, get an answer grounded in that page
- **Contextual word meanings**: not a dictionary definition, but what the word means in this specific passage
- **Explain this passage** and **Key points** style actions on any paragraph
- A general model running under the hood: [Qwen 3](https://huggingface.co/Qwen) (either 1.7B or 4B depending on device RAM) via llama.rn, plus a small embedding model ([EmbeddingGemma](https://huggingface.co/google/embeddinggemma-300m)) for semantic context

The critical word here is **on-device**. Most apps marketed as "AI PDF readers" send your PDF content to a cloud model. ChatPDF, Humata, and similar cloud-first readers all operate this way. Your pages become API calls. The vendor's privacy policy covers what they do with the content. If you're reading a copyrighted textbook, a private research document, or anything you'd rather not upload, that matters.

ReadYuga runs the model on your phone. No cloud call. No training data pipeline. The app has no account, so the output is not associated with any identity, even internally. On airplane mode the AI still works.

This is the single most defensible difference between ReadYuga and ReadEra. If you don't use AI when you read, it doesn't apply. If you do, ReadYuga is the only Android app shipping serious on-device AI reading features in 2026 that I've found.

## Format support, the honest version

ReadEra opens: EPUB, MOBI, AZW3, FB2, PDF, DJVU, DOC, DOCX, RTF, ODT, TXT, CBR, CBZ, and files from ZIP and RAR archives. On its Premium tier CHM is added. One app for a decade of accumulated downloads.

ReadYuga opens: EPUB, PDF, scanned PDF (via OCR).

If your library is mostly Project Gutenberg EPUBs and modern PDFs, this difference doesn't touch you. If your library is years of Kindle-format MOBI or AZW3 files, old FB2 novels, DJVU scans, or CBR / CBZ comic archives, ReadEra is a one-app solution and ReadYuga is not. You could use calibre to convert on desktop, but that's friction most readers don't want.

This is ReadYuga's clearest limitation against ReadEra. I don't have a workaround to suggest beyond calibre. If MOBI matters to you, install ReadEra.

## Install size and performance

ReadEra installs in the low tens of megabytes. ReadYuga installs at around 108 MB (plus optional download for AI model weights, which can run another 1 to 2 GB depending on which Pro features you enable).

ReadEra is lighter because it does less, and the format parsing it does carries very little model or ML weight. ReadYuga is heavier because it bundles OCR model files, TTS infrastructure, and app logic for all the free-tier features before any Pro content is downloaded.

On a phone from the last three years, both apps feel fast. On a very old phone (2 GB RAM, 32 GB storage, Android 10), ReadEra will be more comfortable; ReadYuga's on-device AI features are not realistic on that class of device anyway.

If you have unlimited phone storage, the size difference is a non-issue. If you're on a 32 GB phone with Google Photos backup paused because storage is full, ReadEra is the more considerate neighbour.

## Monetization: both one-time, no subscription

Both apps avoid the modern reader-app pattern of pushing you into a monthly subscription. Both offer a single one-time upgrade and leave it alone.

**ReadEra Premium** is $14.99 as a one-time purchase, with a 30-day money-back guarantee. It adds Google Drive sync (books, progress, bookmarks, quotes across devices), background TTS, imported fonts, page thumbnails for faster navigation, and CHM format support. ReadEra's own developer has said no advertising and no in-app purchases beyond that, as a policy.

**ReadYuga Pro** is around $7.99 as a one-time purchase (localised to each market). It unlocks on-device AI, neural voices, premium themes, PDF signing and form-fill, PDF tools, and quote card exports, plus future Pro features at no additional cost. The free tier is complete enough to live in for months before deciding whether Pro is worth it.

Neither app has ads. Neither has a subscription. If you're coming from Audible, Scribd, or any reader with "unlimited" in its name, this pricing model will feel almost suspicious. It is not. It is what Android reading looked like before 2019, and it is what both apps consciously preserve.

## Cloud sync and cross-device

ReadEra Premium syncs via Google Drive: books, documents, reading progress, bookmarks, and quotes. Users report this works well most of the time, with occasional device-specific issues (one report on a Galaxy Tab S10 Ultra where sync corrupted EPUB rendering; the user resolved it by disabling sync on that device). For most phones, Premium sync is reliable.

ReadYuga does not do cloud sync, by design. There is no account to sign into. If you move to a new phone, you re-import your books from your device storage or restore from a local backup. BYO-cloud sync (point ReadYuga at your own Google Drive or similar) is in the long-term roadmap but has not shipped.

If syncing a library across two or three devices is important to you, ReadEra Premium is the better choice today. If you read on one phone and that's where your library lives, both apps are equivalent on this axis.

## Privacy stance

ReadEra does not require an account. Does not advertise. Stores reading data locally by default. Premium sync is to your own Google Drive, not to ReadEra's servers. Their stance is consistent with their broader "simple reader, no funny business" posture.

ReadYuga does not require an account. Does not advertise. Does not do cloud sync at all (local-only, by design). On-device AI means passages do not travel to any vendor. On-device OCR means scanned textbook pages do not travel either. Privacy-first positioning is not a marketing bullet here; it's the architecture that produces the specific features.

Both apps are good privacy citizens. ReadEra's privacy story is "we don't collect data because we're a simple reader." ReadYuga's is "we don't collect data because we do everything on your device, including the AI." Both are valid; the second has a wider feature surface at the same privacy bar.

## Which to pick, by reader type

**I want a free, ad-free reader for a wide mix of formats, no extra features.**
ReadEra. Install it. Close this tab. Go read.

**I want on-device AI that works on scanned textbooks and lets me ask questions about the pages.**
ReadYuga. The on-device model plus free unlimited OCR is the exact thing it's built for.

**I want neural voices for my commute.**
ReadYuga with Pro. Close to Audible-quality narration, no streaming, no subscription. If you're happy with system TTS and want background playback when the screen locks, ReadEra Premium is the cheaper path.

**I'm a language learner.**
ReadYuga free tier. The offline dictionary plus ML Kit translation plus vocabulary review are hard to assemble out of any other app without stitching four tools together.

**I have a library full of MOBI, AZW3, DJVU, or FB2 files from the Kindle era or from Russian-language collections.**
ReadEra. ReadYuga doesn't open those.

**I'm a university student reading scanned textbook PDFs.**
ReadYuga. On-device OCR, tap-to-define, highlighting, and page Q&A turn a scan into a workable study document.

**I read on an iPhone and an Android phone.**
ReadEra. ReadYuga is Android-only for now.

**I want to sync my library and reading progress across devices.**
ReadEra Premium (Google Drive). ReadYuga doesn't do this yet.

**I'm honestly not sure. I want to try one and see.**
ReadEra first. If after two weeks you keep wishing for dictionary tap, OCR on scans, or something that answers questions about what you're reading, add ReadYuga.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ReadEra better than ReadYuga?

For plain reading across many formats, with mature library management, integrated TTS, and an iOS version, yes. ReadEra is tighter and supports more formats. For reading plus on-device AI, free unlimited OCR, neural voices, and built-in dictionary and translation and vocabulary tools, ReadYuga is more capable, especially in the free tier. Neither is universally better; it depends on what you want the reader to do.

### Does ReadEra have AI?

No. ReadEra is a pure reader without AI features as of 2026. It focuses on reading well and nothing else. ReadYuga is the Android reader shipping on-device AI (chapter summaries, passage Q&A, contextual word meanings) with a local Qwen 3 model, no cloud calls.

### Does ReadEra have text-to-speech?

Yes. ReadEra has built-in TTS using your device's system voices, with speed and repeat controls. Background TTS (audio keeps playing when the screen locks) is a ReadEra Premium feature. ReadYuga also has TTS (system voices in free, neural voices via Piper in Pro).

### Can ReadYuga open MOBI files?

No. ReadYuga focuses on EPUB and PDF (including scanned PDFs via OCR). If you have a MOBI or AZW3 library from the Kindle era, ReadEra or Librera Pro will serve you better. Conversion to EPUB via calibre on desktop also works but is clunky.

### Which app has cloud sync?

ReadEra Premium syncs via Google Drive (books, progress, bookmarks, quotes). ReadYuga does not have cloud sync at all; it is local-only by design. If cross-device sync is a requirement, ReadEra Premium is the choice.

### Which one is better for scanned PDFs?

ReadYuga, clearly. It detects scanned PDFs on import and offers free unlimited on-device OCR to make the text searchable, highlightable, and audibly readable. ReadEra displays scanned PDFs as images with no text extraction, which is correct for a pure reader but not useful if you need to engage with the text.

### Are ReadEra and ReadYuga both really free?

Yes, both have genuinely usable free tiers. ReadEra is free with an optional one-time Premium upgrade at $14.99 (with a 30-day money-back guarantee); many ReadEra users never buy Premium because the free version is already complete. ReadYuga is free with an optional one-time Pro unlock around $7.99 for premium features. Neither has ads, subscriptions, or forced accounts.

### Can I use both apps on the same phone?

Yes, and a non-trivial number of readers do. The usual pattern: ReadEra for the main library (especially MOBI, DJVU, FB2, CBR, and casual novels), ReadYuga for the studied PDFs and anything where you want on-device AI, OCR, or a neural voice. They don't conflict; they read the same files from your phone's storage.

### Is ReadYuga available on iOS?

Not yet. As of November 2026, ReadYuga is Android-only, with iOS on the roadmap but no public date. ReadEra is available on the App Store, so for an iPhone it is the practical answer in this comparison.

## The honest conclusion

ReadEra and ReadYuga occupy different parts of the Android reader space and are both better than most of the alternatives. They are not direct replacements for each other. ReadEra is a near-perfect simple reader with wide format support, mature TTS, and an iOS version. ReadYuga is a feature-dense reader focused on EPUB and PDF, with on-device AI, free unlimited OCR, and neural voices that no other Android reader in 2026 bundles together.

If I had to pick one and only one for a general adult reader who reads mostly novels: ReadEra. It will cover 90% of reading well, be free forever if you want, and never ask anything of you. For a student, researcher, language learner, or anyone who wants to read with AI help or neural audio: ReadYuga. The free tier alone is more capable than most paid readers, and the one-time Pro unlock replaces three or four separate app subscriptions.

If you can install both and let them coexist on your phone, that is probably the right answer. They don't compete with each other so much as cover different parts of what a reader can be.
